Happy Teachers' Day

For every teacher, lecturer and professor who made a subject finally make sense — and stayed a little longer to be sure of it.

read on

Dear Teacher,

Before we understood anything on our own, you explained it first — a formula that looked impossible, a proof that refused to click, a concept we were sure we'd never get. You found the angle that finally made it make sense, and you found it again the second and third time we asked. Every doubt you cleared, every question you didn't rush past, was never really about finishing the syllabus. It was you saying, quietly: I know you can understand this. And, somehow, we always could.

On the day the calendar sets aside for teachers, we wanted to write back — to every teacher, lecturer and professor who ever stayed a few minutes longer to be sure we understood.

Still learning from you,

What you gave us, besides the syllabus

Concepts

Not just formulas — the reasons they hold true, explained until they finally stuck.

Curiosity

The question that mattered more than the answer key, and the room to ask it.

Patience

Explaining it again, a different way, without ever making us feel slow for asking.

Confidence

Walking into a lab, a viva, an exam hall — a little less afraid than before.

Doubts

Office hours that ran long, because our questions did too.

Feedback

Every correction was never a verdict. It was an invitation to try the problem again.

Notes we never sent at the time

“I still hear you walking through that proof, step by step, whenever I get stuck on something completely different now.”

— a student, years later

“You explained it a third time without ever making me feel behind for asking. That stayed with me longer than the answer did.”

— a student, years later

“Every hard problem I've solved since, I first learned how to face one in your class.”

— a student, years later

Because of you, on any ordinary day

We ask the question instead of staying quiet and confused.

We show our working, even the messy middle steps.

We know the difference between memorizing an answer and understanding one.

We try the next approach when the first one doesn't work.

We remember that the subject was never just a subject. It was a way of thinking.

To every teacher, lecturer and professor,

Thank you for explaining it again, and then once more, until it finally made sense. Happy Teachers' Day — today, and on every ordinary day this letter is proof you shaped.
